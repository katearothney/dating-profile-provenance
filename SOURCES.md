# Sources and evidence base

Every empirical claim in this repo is cited and graded. This is an emerging, mixed field. There is strong experimental evidence for a few narrow things (people cannot detect AI faces, watermarks are removable), while the headline worry (AI is making dating measurably more dangerous) rests mostly on totals that do not separate AI from ordinary fraud. Counter-evidence is included on purpose.

Each entry has the specific finding, a grade, and a neutral canonical link where one exists. Where a source is a company or vendor, I say so and grade accordingly.

## The frame (structure of the matrix)
- **Toma and Hancock (2010).** Looks and Lies. Communication Research. The source for treating visual and textual deception as separate channels. Framework, not evidence. [doi.org/10.1177/0093650209356437](https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650209356437)
- **Ellison, Heino and Gibbs (2006).** Managing Impressions Online. JCMC. Profiles as managed self-presentation. Framework. [doi.org/10.1111/j.1083-6101.2006.00020.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1083-6101.2006.00020.x)
- **NIST (2024).** Reducing Risks Posed by Synthetic Content. AI 100-4. The source for the intervention columns (provenance tracking, detection, labeling). Framework plus evidence on limits. [nvlpubs.nist.gov](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-4.pdf)

## Identity verification and the badge
- **Hinge Help.** What is Selfie Verification. Liveness plus 3D face authentication against profile photos, "doesn't guarantee the safety of a particular user." **Strong** (primary). [help.hinge.co](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/10303221435539-What-is-Selfie-Verification)
- **Tinder Help.** How Photo Verification works. Confirms a live person matching profile photos, "doesn't guarantee the identity or safety of a particular user." **Strong** (primary). [help.tinder.com](https://www.help.tinder.com/hc/en-us/articles/4422771431309-How-Does-Photo-Verification-Work)
- **Tinder press room (2025).** Face Check expansion, adds duplicate-face detection across accounts. **Strong** (primary). [tinderpressroom.com](https://www.tinderpressroom.com/2025-10-22-Tinder-to-Expand-Facial-Verification-Feature-Across-the-U-S-,-Setting-a-New-Standard-for-Dating-Safety)
- **ConsumerAffairs (2026).** Selfie verification "validates only a single moment" and does not confirm whether photos "are authentic, AI-generated, or stolen" (quoting SEON). **Moderate** (single outlet, named expert). [consumeraffairs.com](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/tinders-verification-tool-isnt-foolproof-heres-why-scammers-are-still-slipping-through-042426.html)
- **Match Group (2025).** Reality Defender partnership. Reported 99.4 percent of a sample showed no concerning AI manipulation and over 88 percent of flags were ordinary filters. **Contested** as an inference (vendor self-report, no published method). [mtch.com](https://mtch.com/single-news/1022/)
- **Digital Rights Watch (2026).** Biometric retention ambiguity (24-hour deletion versus lifetime FaceMap retention), Match Group market concentration, Grindr AI trained on billions of messages. **Moderate** (advocacy analysis, cites primary pages). [digitalrightswatch.org.au](https://digitalrightswatch.org.au/2026/05/13/too-much-information-dating-apps-and-ai/)
- **EFF (2025).** 10 Dangers of Age Verification. ~15M US adults without a license, 43 percent of trans Americans without correct-gender ID, demographic bias in face estimation. **Strong** (sourced advocacy, hard figures). [eff.org](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/12/10-not-so-hidden-dangers-age-verification)

## Human detection of synthetic images
- **Ivan (2025).** Visual deception in online dating. 831 daters, AI-image detection 42.8 percent (below chance), real 82.7 percent, women better on AI but higher false alarms on real. **Strong** (preregistered experiment, on-point). [doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100208](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100208)
- **Nightingale and Farid (2022).** AI faces indistinguishable from real and rated more trustworthy. 48.2 percent accuracy, 7.7 percent trust gap. **Strong** (PNAS, controlled). [doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2120481119](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2120481119)
- **Frank et al. (2024).** Representative multi-country study, human detection of generated media near chance. **Strong** (IEEE S&P). [doi.org/10.1109/SP54263.2024.00058](https://doi.org/10.1109/SP54263.2024.00058)

## Provenance and watermarking
- **C2PA FAQ and Explainer.** Credentials verify provenance integrity not truth, and a manifest "can be separated" from the asset. **Strong** (primary standard). [c2pa.org/faqs](https://c2pa.org/faqs/)
- **World Privacy Forum (2025).** Forged content can carry a valid signature, capture metadata can deanonymize creators. **Moderate to Strong** (analytical, includes primary quotes). [worldprivacyforum.org](https://www.worldprivacyforum.org/2025/06/report-privacy-identity-and-trust-in-c2pa/)
- **DeepMind SynthID.** Survives routine edits, "isn't foolproof against extreme image manipulations," marks participating models only. **Moderate** (vendor, credible on routine robustness). [deepmind.google](https://deepmind.google/models/synthid/)
- **Dathathri et al. (2024).** SynthID-Text, scalable, negligible quality loss over ~20M responses, weakened by paraphrase. **Strong** (Nature, peer-reviewed). [doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08025-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08025-4)
- **Zhao et al. (2024).** Invisible image watermarks "provably removable" via regeneration attack. **Strong** (NeurIPS). [arxiv.org/abs/2306.01953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01953)
- **Saberi et al. (2024).** Fundamental evasion-versus-spoofing trade-off, watermarks removable and forgeable. **Strong** (peer-reviewed). [arxiv.org/abs/2310.00076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00076)

## Automated detection reliability
- **NewsGuard (2026).** Five detectors, 13.3 percent of authentic images misflagged, worst tool 40 percent. **Moderate** (single small test, n=45). [newsguardtech.com](https://www.newsguardtech.com/special-reports/leading-ai-image-detection-tools-mislead-online-users-often-declaring-authentic-content-fake/)
- **OpenAI (2023).** Shut down its AI-text classifier, 26 percent catch rate, 9 percent false positive. **Strong** (primary admission). [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/)
- **fotto.ai (2026).** Vendor selling AI dating photos concedes detection is "strong against deception and weak against honesty." **Moderate** (industry blog, useful admission-against-interest). [fotto.ai](https://fotto.ai/blog/can-tinder-detect-ai-photos)

- **Barkallah and Zytko (2026).** 113 daters, AI used to fabricate personalities, seldom disclosed, framed as a possible sexual-assault risk factor via misinformed consent. **Moderate** (rich qualitative, self-reported). [doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791593](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791593)

## Does labeling work, or backfire
- **Pennycook et al. (2020).** Implied truth effect. Warning some fakes raised belief in unlabeled fakes, sharing of untagged false headlines rose from 29.8 to 36.2 percent. n>5000. **Moderate** (large, preregistered, but survey paradigm). [doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3478](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3478)
- **Altay and Gilardi (2024).** "AI-generated" label lowers perceived accuracy for true and human-made content, about a fifth of a point on a six-point scale. n~4976. **Moderate** (preregistered, cross-national). [doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae403](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae403)
- **Martel and Rand (2024).** Meta-analysis, warning labels cut false-belief 27.6 percent and sharing 24.7 percent, and worked even for people who distrust fact-checkers. **Strong** (meta-analysis, 21 experiments). This is the check on the two above. [doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01973-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-01973-x)
- **Sadasivan et al. (2023).** As generators improve, best-possible detector AUROC collapses toward chance, paraphrase defeats detectors. **Moderate to Contested** (strong result, debated assumptions). [arxiv.org/abs/2303.11156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11156)
- **Popular AI (2026).** Argues provenance plus identity plus ranking can turn a context label into a gatekeeping "permission system," and that C2PA proves a chain, not truth. **Emerging** (opinion analysis, well-argued, not empirical). Useful as the civil-liberties counter to my own position. [popularai.org](https://www.popularai.org/p/ai-provenance-creator-permission-system)

## Regulation
- **EU AI Act, Article 50 (Reg 2024/1689).** Providers must mark AI output machine-readable, deployers must disclose deepfakes, applies from 2 August 2026. **Strong** (enacted). [eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) and the Commission FAQ [digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)
- **California SB 942 (amended by AB 853).** Covered-provider watermarking and detection tools, operative 2 August 2026, duty on the AI provider not the host. **Strong** (enacted). [Morgan Lewis summary](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/new-california-ai-disclosure-rules-become-operative)
- **TAKE IT DOWN Act (2025).** Federal removal duty for nonconsensual intimate imagery including AI forgeries, 48-hour takedown, FTC enforcement from May 2026. Removal, not labeling. **Strong** (enacted). [ftc.gov](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/05/take-it-down-act-enforcement-starts-now-what-know-about-ftc-tida)
