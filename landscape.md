# How AI shows up in dating apps

The provenance question is one corner of a much bigger picture. AI is now woven through the whole dating experience, and not all of it is bad. This page maps where it shows up, what it might be good for, and where the risk sits. I try to keep both columns honest, because a threat-only version of this would be dishonest, and a hype version would be worse.

This page is context, not the ask. The repo's actual demand is scoped to content provenance for images, video, and voice or audio (see [PROPOSAL.md](PROPOSAL.md)). Everything below is the wider landscape that surrounds that narrow, buildable request.

This is a living map. Grades are lighter here because a lot of it is too new to have real evidence yet.

## Matching and recommendation
- **What it is.** The core algorithms that decide who you see. Match Group's leadership has said AI will influence "everything from profile creation to matching and connecting for dates" ([Guardian via Digital Rights Watch, 2026](https://digitalrightswatch.org.au/2026/05/13/too-much-information-dating-apps-and-ai/)). Tinder demoed "Chemistry," which scans your camera roll (with permission) and runs interactive Q and As to curate daily recommendations ([GeekWire, 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/ai-enters-the-chat-new-seattle-dating-app-relies-on-tech-to-facilitate-meaningful-human-connections/)). Facebook Dating, now at 21 million daily users and larger than Hinge, added an AI dating assistant and an AI matchmaker called "Meet Cute" that serves one match a week to cut swipe fatigue ([New York Times, 2025](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/11/03/technology/facebook-dating-surprise-hit.html)).
- **Possible good.** Better matching could cut swipe fatigue and surface more compatible people.
- **The risk.** Ranking by "desirability" can entrench bias, and reporting suggests people of color receive less engagement and can be scored and shown a narrower pool as a result ([Digital Rights Watch, 2026](https://digitalrightswatch.org.au/2026/05/13/too-much-information-dating-apps-and-ai/)). Camera-roll scanning is a large new data grab. And the business runs on engagement, so an algorithm that keeps you swiping is not always one that helps you leave. **Emerging.**

## Profile and photo assistants
- **What it is.** Tools that write or polish your bio and generate or enhance your photos. Bumble launched AI profile guidance, Facebook Dating added an AI assistant, and a whole third-party market exists (Pose AI, fotto.ai, and others) selling "identity-locked" AI dating photos ([CBC, 2026](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/ai-online-dating-9.7145862), [Pose AI, 2026](https://pose.ai/blog/are-ai-photos-allowed-dating-apps-2026)).
- **Possible good.** A well-lit, well-written profile genuinely helps, and these tools remove the disadvantage of not owning a good camera or being a confident writer.
- **The risk.** This is the provenance problem itself. The line between "my best self" and "not me" is exactly where consent breaks, and the whole rest of this repo lives here. **Moderate.**

## Message assistants
- **What it is.** ChatGPT, plus purpose-built apps like Rizz and Wingman, drafting openers and replies. Hinge added US "Convo Starters" that suggest a topic from a match's profile. This has a name now, "chatfishing," and it is common: a McAfee survey found 81 percent of Indian respondents reported better responses with AI-generated content, and a Norton study found six in ten daters believe they have hit at least one AI-written conversation ([Times of India, 2026](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/events/hyderabad/ai-in-romance-how-gen-z-uses-ai-for-dating-app-messages-and-love-notes/articleshow/117314260.cms), [Indian Express, 2026](https://indianexpress.com/article/lifestyle/feelings/ai-flirt-dating-apps-psychologist-warns-hidden-emotional-cost-chatfishing-10442867/)).
- **Possible good.** Real help for people who freeze at the blank message box, and genuine accessibility value for neurodivergent and disabled daters ([CBC, 2026](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/ai-online-dating-9.7145862)).
- **The risk.** This is where the deception research bites hardest. AI-written messages reduce trust in the author when noticed ([Wu and Kelly, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3441000.3441074)), and are used to fabricate personalities that do not survive a real date ([Barkallah and Zytko, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791593)). One woman described three weeks of thoughtful Hinge messages collapsing on the date into "knock, knock, is anyone home," the AI having secured the meeting the man could not sustain himself ([The Guardian via Futurism, 2025](https://futurism.com/chatgpt-dating-advice-men)). It also homogenizes everyone into the same voice, which people find flattening and off-putting. **Moderate.**

## AI as matchmaker (a design response)
- **What it is.** Apps that lean into AI to replace swiping. Keeper.AI vets human-curated matches, and Seattle's Lamu withholds photos until after an AI-mediated conversation, so people connect on words first ([GeekWire, 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/ai-enters-the-chat-new-seattle-dating-app-relies-on-tech-to-facilitate-meaningful-human-connections/)).
- **Possible good.** Withholding photos until later is an interesting answer to visual deception, because it moves the first impression off the image entirely. A small registration fee is also used to scare off fake profiles.
- **The risk.** It hands even more of the intimate judgment to an algorithm, and "efficiency" is not obviously what love needs ([Harvard Gazette, 2025](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2025/02/tech-has-changed-dating-its-complicated/)). **Emerging.**

## AI companions
- **What it is.** Romantic or flirtatious chatbots inside dating apps. Grindr built an "AI wingman" described as "surprisingly flirtatious," reportedly trained on billions of private messages and heading toward a paid "AI boyfriend" ([Digital Rights Watch, 2026](https://digitalrightswatch.org.au/2026/05/13/too-much-information-dating-apps-and-ai/)).
- **Possible good.** Company for lonely people, and a low-stakes place to practice. The first large-scale study of an AI-companion community (27,000 members) found 25.4 percent of posts reported a clear net life benefit against 3.0 percent net harm, with users citing reduced loneliness and mental-health support ([Pataranutaporn et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11391)).
- **The risk.** The same study found 9.5 percent acknowledging emotional dependency, 4.6 percent reality dissociation, and grief that reads like bereavement when a model updates, alongside a documented teen-suicide case tied to a companion chatbot ([Pataranutaporn et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11391)). A separate study of 30 users in AI romances names the pattern the "AI Amplifier Effect," where the AI magnifies whatever emotional state the user brings, healing for some and isolating for others ([Pang et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08084)). The stakes are highest for minors: a Common Sense Media and Stanford investigation found it took very little prompting for companion chatbots to engage teens in harmful conversations, and both Common Sense Media and the APA now advise against under-18s using AI companions ([APA, 2025](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2025/10/technology-youth-friendships)). Separately, intimate disclosures to a chatbot (health, sexuality, politics) can be retained and sold, and 80 percent of dating apps share or sell user data. This is a privacy problem wearing a romance costume. **Moderate on the companionship findings, Emerging on the dating-app-specific privacy claim.**

## Safety uses (the good column, mostly)
- **What it is.** AI aimed at protecting users rather than optimizing engagement. Bumble's "Deception Detector" reportedly blocked 95 percent of accounts identified as spam or scams, Tinder's "Are You Sure?" uses context-aware AI to blur harassing messages before they are seen, and Match Group screens profile images for AI manipulation with Reality Defender ([GeekWire, 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/ai-enters-the-chat-new-seattle-dating-app-relies-on-tech-to-facilitate-meaningful-human-connections/), [Match Group, 2025](https://mtch.com/single-news/1022/)).
- **Possible good.** This is AI working for safety, and behavioral detection catches coordinated scams that photo forensics miss.
- **The risk.** The numbers are self-reported by the companies, "95 percent of identified accounts" quietly depends on what got identified, and none of these tools surface their results to the user or touch the provenance gap. **Moderate on the concept, Contested on the specific figures.**

## The cross-domain pattern
This is not only a dating problem. The same authenticity crisis is showing up in hiring, where AI-generated applications have pushed some recruiters to source candidates in person and to ask applicants to disclose how much AI they used. The tell is the same everywhere: once a tool can imitate a person well, "did a human really do this" becomes a question we do not have good infrastructure to answer. Dating is just where it hurts most, because the stakes are physical safety and consent, not a resume.
