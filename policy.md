# The policy landscape

What the law actually does here, and what it does not. The short answer to the question I started with (are they technically required to do this) is no. No law today requires a dating app to label AI-generated profile photos. The transparency duties that exist point at the makers of the AI, not the platforms that host it. This page lays that out and then sketches the policy options, with the honest tradeoffs attached. It is a living page.

## What is already law
- **EU AI Act, Article 50.** From 2 August 2026, providers of generative AI must mark their output as machine-readable and detectable as AI, and deployers must disclose deepfakes. It applies to whoever generates or deploys the content, so a dating app is only directly covered if it runs its own generative feature ([Reg 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj), [EC FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)). **Strong** (enacted, in force).
- **California SB 942 (with AB 853).** From 2 August 2026, large generative-AI providers must offer a detection tool and embed provenance data. The duty is on the AI provider, not the host ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/new-california-ai-disclosure-rules-become-operative)). **Strong**.
- **TAKE IT DOWN Act (US, 2025).** Requires covered platforms to remove nonconsensual intimate imagery, including AI forgeries, within 48 hours, FTC-enforced from May 2026. This one does reach a dating app, but it is a removal duty for intimate-image abuse, not a labeling duty ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/05/take-it-down-act-enforcement-starts-now-what-know-about-ftc-tida)). **Strong**.
- **UK Online Safety Act (2023).** Made sharing digitally manipulated explicit images illegal, though UN Women notes it does not clearly cover creating deepfakes or sharing where intent to cause distress cannot be proved ([UN Women, 2025](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/ai-powered-online-abuse-how-ai-is-amplifying-violence-against-women-and-what-can-stop-it)). **Strong** on existence, with a named gap.
- **Mexico's Ley Olimpia and similar laws** across Latin America criminalize digital sexual violence, with Argentina, Panama, and Uruguay expected to follow ([UN Women, 2025](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/ai-powered-online-abuse-how-ai-is-amplifying-violence-against-women-and-what-can-stop-it)). **Moderate** (cited via UN Women).
- **Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI.** A treaty-level model for requiring AI to meet safety and ethics standards before public rollout, offered by UN Women as a coordination template ([UN Women, 2025](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/ai-powered-online-abuse-how-ai-is-amplifying-violence-against-women-and-what-can-stop-it)). **Moderate**.

## The structural gap
Every enacted transparency duty lands on the generator or the deployer of the AI, and dating apps mostly sit downstream of that as hosts. So a compliant AI image should in principle arrive already marked, which a dating app could choose to surface. Nothing yet requires the app to read that mark, to label the photo, or to run its own detection. Less than half of countries even have laws prosecuting online abuse, and where they exist, enforcement is weak ([UN Women, 2025](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/ai-powered-online-abuse-how-ai-is-amplifying-violence-against-women-and-what-can-stop-it)). That is the gap this repo lives in.

## Policy options, with the tradeoff attached
- **Mandate provenance at generation.** Require synthetic-media tools to attach verifiable C2PA-style credentials, which a UN Women policy expert argues would let platforms and investigators detect origin and make perpetrators unable to plausibly deny it ([UN Women, 2025](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/ai-powered-online-abuse-how-ai-is-amplifying-violence-against-women-and-what-can-stop-it)). Tradeoff: open-source and self-hosted generators route around it, and the mark still strips downstream.
- **Give hosts a duty to surface credentials.** Require large platforms, including dating apps, to read and display provenance when it is present. Tradeoff: it only helps for content that still carries a credential, which excludes screenshots and short text.
- **Require honest labeling design.** If apps label, require them to label true and human content too, not only the AI they catch, to avoid the implied-truth backfire ([Pennycook et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3478)). Tradeoff: more labels everywhere can dull the signal.
- **Privacy-preserving verification, not blanket ID.** Favor on-device and zero-knowledge age and humanity checks over mandatory government ID, to avoid excluding the roughly 15 million US adults without a license and the 43 percent of trans Americans without correct-gender ID ([EFF, 2025](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/12/10-not-so-hidden-dangers-age-verification)). Tradeoff: harder to build, and proof-of-humanity systems like World ID raise their own centralization concerns ([BBC, 2026](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cp9vppem4evo)).
- **Duties on tech companies directly.** UN Women's list: make "nudify" tools inaccessible, refuse to host their output, build fast reporting and removal, auto-check for watermarks, and mandate tagging of AI content ([UN Women, 2025](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/ai-powered-online-abuse-how-ai-is-amplifying-violence-against-women-and-what-can-stop-it)). Tradeoff: enforcement across borders is the hard part, and detection is imperfect.

## The strongest argument against all of this
Provenance plus identity plus ranking can slide from a context label into a permission system that quietly decides who is visible, hitting anonymous and marginalized creators first, and every layer can stay formally optional while refusing a credential becomes commercially impossible ([Popular AI, 2026](https://www.popularai.org/p/ai-provenance-creator-permission-system)). I take this seriously. It is why I keep landing on labeling and disclosure rather than gatekeeping, and why the design of any mandate matters as much as the mandate.

On the common worry that regulating AI kills innovation, a UN Women policy expert calls the regulation-versus-innovation framing "a falsehood learned from the unregulated evolution of social media," arguing that governance channels innovation rather than banning it ([UN Women, 2025](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/ai-powered-online-abuse-how-ai-is-amplifying-violence-against-women-and-what-can-stop-it)). I find that persuasive, and I am also aware it is a view, not a settled fact, so I hold it as one.
