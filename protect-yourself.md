# Using the tech you have right now

The [proposal](PROPOSAL.md) is about what the apps should do. This page is about what you can do today, while they catch up. I want to be honest up front: none of this is reliable, and that is exactly the point. The fact that spotting AI content now takes a checklist and a live-video interrogation is the argument for putting provenance labels on the apps, so the burden stops falling on the person being deceived. Until then, here is what current tools actually get you, and where each one fails.

## For photos

Reverse image search still helps, but only halfway. Running a photo through a reverse image search catches a scammer who stole a real person's pictures from the web. It does nothing against a fully AI-generated face, because there is no original to find, so a clean result is not proof the person is real. Treat a hit as a red flag and a miss as nothing.

The visual tells are aging fast. Warped backgrounds, a melting light switch, distorted text on a sign, too-perfect skin, and hands with the wrong number of fingers are still real giveaways when a model gets sloppy. But the models are fixing these quickly, which researchers call the "learning loop," so anything you learned to look for last year is less reliable this year. Use the tells, do not trust them.

Detection apps exist and are imperfect. Consumer tools like Trend Micro Check and various online detectors can give you a second opinion, but independent testing shows they wrongly flag real photos and miss polished fakes often enough that you cannot treat their verdict as the answer. A second opinion, not a ruling.

## For video calls

Move to a live call early, and make it interactive. A pre-recorded clip proves nothing, and even a live call is no longer proof on its own, because real-time deepfakes exist. What still trips up current tools is physical occlusion, so on a live call ask them to slowly wave a hand across their face, turn their head 90 degrees, or hold up a random object you name in the moment. These tend to cause glitches. Multiple short calls over time are harder to fake than one long one.

Do not bet your safety on finding the glitch. Forensic experts caution against this directly, because if you look for a tell, fail to find one, and then trust the person, you can be very wrong. Use the challenge as one signal among several, not as a green light.

## For voice and audio

Be skeptical of voice. Voice cloning needs only a few seconds of audio, so a familiar-sounding voice note is not proof of anything, and a voice on a live call can be cloned too. On a dating app you have no shared history to fall back on, so there is no safe word to set up with a stranger. Treat voice as one more thing that can be synthetic, not as confirmation that the person is real.

## The habits that work regardless of the tech

- Stay on the platform. A push to move to WhatsApp or Telegram quickly is a red flag, because scammers want off the app's monitoring.
- Never send money, crypto, gift cards, or verification codes to someone you have not met in person, no matter the emergency.
- Watch the writing. A voice that suddenly turns flowery and poetic, replies that arrive at all hours, and "performative empathy" that mirrors your feelings a little too neatly are chatfishing tells.
- Check the social history. A real person usually has established accounts elsewhere with followers, comments, and history. Brand-new accounts with few followers are burner accounts.
- Slow down. Urgency, secrecy, and strong emotion are the engine of every one of these scams. Pausing to verify independently is the single most effective thing you can do.
- Reduce your own footprint. The fewer clear photos and voice samples of you are public, the less raw material a scammer has to clone you.

## Why this list should not have to exist

Every item here asks you to become a part-time forensic analyst on a first date. That is unreasonable, and it is why the real fix is not a better personal checklist but provenance labeling on the apps themselves. The [proposal](PROPOSAL.md) is the version of this that does not depend on you catching what trained experts cannot. This page is the stopgap.
